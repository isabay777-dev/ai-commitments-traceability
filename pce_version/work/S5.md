The reconstructed population contains 213 operational keys with available later reports: 33 Kazakhstani, 34 Polish and 146 Dutch. Of these, 190 entered the archived initial outcome coding. Six subsequently received a documented `not_a_promise` decision at F3 and one at F4, leaving 183 final records. For the other 23 keys (4 Kazakhstani, 4 Polish and 15 Dutch), the pre-coding omission is observable but its individual reason is unresolved. The historical code can omit both empty verdicts and `not_a_promise` verdicts; this does not identify which mechanism applied to any missing key. None of the 23 omitted keys has been re-adjudicated, restored or assigned an outcome for this ledger.

`selection_ledger_213.csv` is the complete machine-readable ledger. It records each four-part key and reconstructed promise ID, the first retained fragment and its archived excerpt, all candidate fragment IDs, later-report filenames and years, membership at each coding stage, disposition, recorded reason and evidence source. `selection_ledger_unresolved_23.csv` isolates the unknown cases. `selection_ledger_fragment_links.csv` provides one row per candidate-fragment link. `selection_ledger_audit.json` records checks, unresolved IDs and input hashes. All four files are in `analysis/out/` of the local preparation package; this statement does not assert that this version has been publicly released.

Promise IDs are reconstructed from the historical sorted order of the 213 keys. All 184 F3 records match the reconstructed country, firm, category and year, and all 183 final records also match their archived number of later reports. These checks establish consistency with archived analytical files, not independent verification of unique project identity, commitment eligibility or implementation. The retained quotations are retrieval excerpts, not newly verified complete source passages.

Follow-up availability is reconstructed from panel metadata and the historical four-report exclusion list. Historical script 21 checks text-file size in **bytes** (at least 50,000); the reconstruction uses the panel's **character** count (at least 50,000). Those filters are not identical. The count agreement for final records does not establish that all unavailable or excluded source texts have been recovered or re-extracted. No new full-text audit was performed for this ledger reconstruction; the separate first-report exercise is documented in Section S5.13 below.

## Section S5.13. Provisional first-available-report comparison

This extension concerns the original 183-key retained worklist. It uses original report context and the first available subsequent report in the frozen inventory, rather than the full set of later reports used by the original F5 exercise. All sources are public. Frozen source-only independent model rounds were followed by source-based adjudication. These procedures do not constitute independent human validation. Country and source language were evident to the models.

The outcome is confirmation at the original undertaking's AI function, stage and scope. It is not implementation, timeliness, technological success or disclosure quality. Reporting-year gaps do not measure exact exposure. Exact publication dates are not inferred from report-year labels. Cases with uncertain eligibility, entity/perimeter, linkage or adequate context remain unresolved; newly identified non-promises are separately excluded. Eligibility and fresh coding changes mean differences from the original F5 classification cannot be attributed exclusively to the follow-up window.

Structured independent entity/object-linkage agreement was not collected in a common categorical field; source-adequacy and eligibility agreement do not replace it. Narrative linkage checks are retained in adjudication.

The 23 previously unresolved selection omissions are outside this retained-worklist comparison and remain explicitly documented in the selection ledger. These results therefore do not resolve the corpus-selection objection.
